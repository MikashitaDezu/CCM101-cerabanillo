<p align="justify">
Out of the three, AWS is by far the platform I prefer, mainly because of how many services it allocates. What stood out first was the sufficient number of services, more than 200, covering everything from hosting a basic website to building AI applications. It isn't the easiest platform to learn; the acronyms and naming conventions may take some time to get used to, but the documentation suffices. AWS has also been around since 2006, the longest of the three, which explains the data centers and job listings that come with it.
</p>

<p align="justify">
AWS, Azure, and GCP look different on the surface level, but they're built the same way underneath. All three offer virtual machines, object storage, managed databases, identity management, and managed Kubernetes, just under different names. They all bill pay-as-you-go instead of requiring upfront hardware, and they all organize infrastructure into Regions and Zones so one data center failing doesn't take the whole system down. Once that pattern is recognized, a new provider is really just new names formulated for concepts already known.
</p>

<p align="justify">
This mission also showed me that a business's actual needs should drive the platform choice, not reputation. A startup on a tight budget needs something different from a university already invested in Microsoft tools, and different again from a research company that needs raw computing power for AI. The right call depends on what a client already has and where they're headed, not just which provider is most popular.
</p>

<p align="justify">
Acting as a Cloud Solutions Consultant pushed me to think past technical specs. A client's existing tools, budget, and growth plans matter as much as performance numbers, sometimes more. There's no single best platform overall; each one is genuinely strong in a different area, and picking one means picking trade-offs, not a winner.
</p>

<p align="justify">
My GitHub Cloud Computing Portfolio has clearly improved through the work allocated and formulated across this mission checkpoint so far. The Cloud Computing field has taught me that almost anything digital can be allocated, formulated, and organized with the right approach. I'm more comfortable navigating the portfolio now, and it shows in how it's organized; I'll keep learning new things as life and technology progress, with research notes on all three providers, real client recommendations, and a decision matrix that ties it together. It's starting to look less like a school assignment and more like proof I can actually evaluate cloud platforms.
</p>
